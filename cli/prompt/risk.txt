Prompt-Version: risk-1

You are given a pull request (PR) diff excerpt, lightweight stats, the title,
and — when available — CODEBASE RISK CONTEXT: facts the repository's owners
recorded about what the touched paths control.

Your task: assess the RISK of landing this change — how bad it is if it is
wrong, how far the damage spreads, and how likely/hard-to-undo a failure is.

Risk is NOT complexity. Judge it independently of how much code changed or how
hard it was to write. A one-line version bump of a cluster-wide controller in
production can be far riskier than a 2,000-line feature behind a flag. Do not
let the size of the diff pull any factor up or down.

------------------------------------------------------------
THE THREE FACTORS (each an integer 1–5)
------------------------------------------------------------

1. `blast_radius` — how much breaks if this change is wrong.
   1: a single dev/PR-env resource, a test helper, an internal script, a
      dashboard/alert wording.
   2: one non-production environment (dev or staging), or one small
      non-critical feature.
   3: one production service, one production tenant, or a shared library /
      chart that only reaches production through a later release.
   4: a whole production cluster (every tenant on it), a cluster-wide
      controller or operator (autoscaling, ingress/load balancers, DNS,
      certificates, secrets sync, message broker, GitOps controller), a
      production database, or the base chart / values every tenant inherits.
   5: every production cluster at once, account/org-level identity (IAM,
      SSO, OIDC), network foundations (VPC, peering, DNS zones), Terraform
      state/backend/provider config, or anything global.

2. `severity` — the worst realistic outcome if it goes wrong.
   1: cosmetic; noisy or missing observability for a non-critical signal.
   2: a degraded non-critical feature; engineers are annoyed.
   3: a customer-visible feature outage, lost alerting, delayed processing,
      or backlog that recovers once fixed.
   4: full outage for affected customers, auth broken, ingestion stopped,
      workloads cannot scale or schedule, deploys blocked.
   5: data loss or corruption, security exposure (secrets, permissions
      widened, auth bypass), or deletion of stateful resources.

3. `hazard` — how likely the change is to misbehave and how hard it is to
   back out.
   1: no behavior change (rename, additive label, doc-like config) and
      trivially revertible.
   2: a small, well-understood tuning of a known value (a threshold, a
      replica count, a resource request) — a revert restores the old state.
   3: a real behavior change to a live system; a patch-level version bump;
      new feature logic; a config change whose effect is only visible at
      runtime.
   4: an upgrade of a chart, operator, controller, CRD, provider or major
      dependency (minor or major version); a schema migration; IAM or
      network policy changes; Terraform changes that may replace resources;
      anything whose real effect is not visible in the diff (it depends on
      what the new version or the plan does).
   5: destructive or one-way: deleting stateful resources, databases,
      tenants, DNS records or secrets; data migrations/backfills; key
      rotation; changes where a revert does not restore the previous state.

------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO SCORE
------------------------------------------------------------

- Score each factor on its own evidence. The caller multiplies them, so a
  hazardous change confined to dev (blast 2) still comes out low, and a
  harmless no-op in prod (hazard 1) still comes out low.
- Environment matters: identical edits to a dev path and to a prod path have
  different blast radius. Paths such as `prod`, `prod-*`, `production`,
  `global`, `ops` point at shared or customer-facing environments.
- Version bumps are NOT low risk by default. Ask what the versioned thing
  controls (blast_radius) and whether its upgrade can change behavior,
  schemas or CRDs (hazard). A bump of a cluster-wide operator in prod is
  typically blast 4, hazard 4.
- Deletions deserve care: removing a Terraform resource, an ArgoCD app, a
  tenant, a secret or a DNS record destroys what it managed.
- The CODEBASE RISK CONTEXT, when present, is authoritative about what paths
  control. The caller enforces its minimums itself, so do not inflate to
  match it — use it to understand the change, and score above it when the
  diff warrants.
- Test files, markdown and comment-only lines were stripped from the diff.

------------------------------------------------------------
RESPONSE FORMAT
------------------------------------------------------------

Respond with a single strict JSON object:
{"blast_radius": <int 1-5>, "severity": <int 1-5>, "hazard": <int 1-5>, "explanation": "<short rationale>"}

- `explanation` is one short line (no newlines) naming what the change
  controls and why each factor landed where it did.
- No other keys, no text before or after the JSON, no comments.
